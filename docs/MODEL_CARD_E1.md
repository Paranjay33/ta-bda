## Intended use

Research and teaching demonstration of sentence-level 3-class sentiment (negative / neutral / positive) for Romanized Hindi-English code-mixed social-media text, as part of the HinglishSense E1–E4 comparison.

## Out of scope

Decisions about individuals (moderation, hiring, credit, health, legal); measuring anyone's real feelings; Devanagari or non-Hinglish text; long documents; any use that treats the score as ground truth.

## Model

Spark ML Pipeline: RegexTokenizer(\s+, no lowercasing) → CountVectorizer(vocab 20,000) → IDF → LogisticRegression(family=multinomial, maxIter=100, regParam=0). Fitted on the training split only and persisted as a PipelineModel (tokenizer, vocabulary, IDF and classifier together).

Hyperparameters: `lr_reg_param=0.0`, `lr_max_iter=100`, `lr_family=multinomial`, `tokenizer=RegexTokenizer(pattern=\s+, toLowercase=false)`, `word_tfidf_num_features=20000`, `smoke_test=False`

## Scores shown to users

Spark's multinomial probabilities, served as `model_probability`. They are NOT calibrated and, because the model is unregularised, are extremely over-confident (see the probabilistic metrics); the UI labels them 'top class score (uncalibrated)'.

## Limitations and bias

- Bag of words: no word order, so contrast/negation structure is invisible.
- Unregularised (regParam=0, the midterm definition was kept): over-confident probabilities.
- English negators and contrast words (`not`, `no`, `but`) were removed from the data upstream, so no model can use them; Hindi ones (`nahi`, `mat`, `lekin`) remain.
- Tweets from one collection period; political and religious topics are heavily represented, and the neutral class is noisy. Errors are not uniform across topics.
- One split and one seed; no estimate of seed variance.
